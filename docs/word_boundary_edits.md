# Bounded ciphertext boundary edits

Allowing at most two boundary edits produces no candidate in the six-work
medieval Latin sample. All 83 length-compatible alignments under the fixed
seven-unit contraction inventory contradict exact word repetition. No Z3
call is required to exclude these alignments.

## Scope

The fixed 22-word Voynich block contains 104 EVA characters. Characters and
their order remain fixed; only cuts between them change. Inserting or deleting
a cut costs one edit. Moving a boundary costs two edits: one deletion and one
insertion. We exhaustively enumerate layouts with zero, one, or two edits.

Source passages retain their attested word order and boundaries. Their lengths
range from 20 to 24 words. Gaps, chapters and books remain barriers. Sources
are Aquinas, Vita Caroli, Einhard, Gesta Francorum, Philobiblon and Monarchia,
using the extraction and normalization documented in the
[expanded sample](expanded_medieval_latin.md).

The letters profile uses ordinary letters. The bounded-seven profile uses the
unchanged [contraction inventory](latin_contractions.md), with optional,
non-overlapping applications. For every attainable unit count k, an emitted
word may contain k through 3k characters. We test the union of these intervals,
without requiring maximal contraction.

## Results

Available source spans number 80,378, 80,201, 80,024, 79,847 and 79,670 for
20, 21, 22, 23 and 24 words respectively: 400,120 overlapping spans per profile.
These overlapping spans are not independent observations.

| Profile | Zero-edit length fits | One-edit length fits | Two-edit length fits | Repetition-compatible fits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Letters | 0 | 1 | 77 | 0 |
| Seven historical units | 0 | 1 | 82 | 0 |

Counts are alignments: different boundary layouts can fit the same source
passage. Across edit costs, the letters profile has 13 distinct length-fitting
source spans and the seven-unit profile has 14. Aquinas contributes one
two-edit alignment per profile; Dante contributes all other alignments.

The sole one-edit length fit deletes the cut between `daiin` and `cthy`,
creating `daiincthy`. Its 21-word Dante passage is:

```text
cum omne quod in mente dei est sit deus iuxta illud quod factum est in ipso vita erat et deus maxime
```

But the unchanged ciphertext word `chol` at positions 2 and 12 would decode
to `omne` and `quod`. Likewise, `chor` at positions 6 and 15 would decode to
`dei` and `in`, and `cham` at positions 9 and 18 to `deus` and `erat`.
Every other length-fitting alignment also has a saved repetition contradiction.

A globally uniquely decodable key with reversible contraction expansion must
decode equal complete ciphertext words to equal source words. These
contradictions therefore exclude the candidates even with free homophone
selection. We do not solve global codebook feasibility or cycling constraints.
The result excludes these sampled passages within the two-edit scope; it does
not exclude Latin generally or larger boundary changes.

## Reproduction and validation

Run the solver-free analysis against the existing cached sources:

```sh
.venv/bin/python -m benchmarks.word_boundary_edits
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests/integration/test_word_boundary_edits.py
```

The 23 boundary tests include comparisons against independent brute-force cut
enumeration, moved-boundary costs, joins/splits, repetition and source barriers.
The combined focused analysis suites passed 74 tests.

Artifacts are [report.json](../experiments/results/word-boundary-edits/report.json),
12 adjacent alignment JSONL files, and
[validation.json](../experiments/results/word-boundary-edits/validation.json).
The report records source manifests and inventory, baseline, code, normalized
source and alignment hashes. Validation independently reconstructs every saved
layout, checks edit costs and emission lengths, and verifies every repetition
certificate. The validation artifact applies to the saved report hash; it is
not regenerated by the analysis command.
