# Progress: 2026-10-24

Three conditional grammatical findings may supply useful information to a future Voynich decipherment algorithm, even though they have not produced reliable lexical nuclei. Preserve them independently of whether the current decoder can exploit them. The overarching objective and working assumptions are in [high_level_goal.md](../high_level_goal.md).

## 1. Opaque function-word classification

A frozen model distinguishes prepositions (ADP) from coordinating conjunctions (CCONJ) for repeated opaque word types. On the cached source-test text, it correctly classifies **12/16 types (75%)**, versus **8/16** for raw-model and prior baselines, with **6/8 correct in each class**. Without refitting, expanded cached contexts yield **11/16 correct for Jerome (68.75%)** and **13/15 for Caesar (86.67%)**, versus eight correct for the respective baselines.

All sixteen target types are excluded from model training labels, and their identities and classes are masked during inference. However, the function-word candidate catalogue is supplied, as are surrounding anchor mappings. This is conditional binary type classification, not general token POS tagging or blind discovery of function words. Short-context transfer is uneven: Jerome's original 100 sentences yield 7/13 correct, below the prior's 8/13. References are supplied modal type classes, not token-level POS annotations.

**Potential decipherment input:** probabilistic grammatical classes for repeated cipher words, usable as constraints or features in another decoder if the required surrounding information can be obtained.

Evidence: [iteration 118](../grammar_class_validation_iteration118.md), [author transfer, iteration 119](../grammar_class_transfer_iteration119.md).

## 2. Structural PP membership from inferred opaque classes

With all target lexical identities hidden, passing inferred grammatical classes to the frozen PP-membership model increases correct selections from **23 to 235**, with false selections rising from **0 to 34**. The inferred-class result has **87.36% precision**. On the fixed originally unmapped-word cohort, correct selections rise from **11 to 174**, with **33 false selections (84.06% precision)**. Thus the class signal has measurable downstream structural value beyond class-label accuracy.

Surrounding anchor mappings remain supplied, and the test data have been reused in development. This does not recover full grammatical structure or establish blind Voynich applicability. The primary 90% precision requirement is unmet. A subsequent exhaustive diagnostic shows that a global ADP-confidence cutoff cannot satisfy both 90% precision and retention of 80% of correct selections with these fixed scores and models; this failure does not erase the underlying structural gain.

**Potential decipherment input:** soft evidence that an opaque token belongs to a prepositional phrase, which another decoder could combine with lexical, morphological, or positional constraints.

Evidence: [iteration 120](../grammar_class_pp_utility_iteration120.md), [scalar-gate limitation, iteration 123](../grammar_adp_gate_ceiling_iteration123.md).

## 3. Selective repair of corrupted supplied anchor classes

A frozen omission-aware repair policy transfers to cached Jerome context and makes **25 correct repairs, zero wrong repairs, and 39 abstentions** across **64 synthetic cross-class swaps**. Every successful repair restores the complete clean PP probability vector on the scored sample. The experiment uses 829 sentences of context while scoring the original 100 sentences; the policy also abstains on all three clean/same-class controls.

These are correlated synthetic corruption cases, not independent texts or recovered plaintext word identities. The procedure starts with largely correct supplied anchor mappings and a candidate catalogue. Restoring clean PP predictions does not make those predictions perfect: the clean scored reference has 40 correct and one wrong selected PP node. Subsequent literal-absence/context-loss tests expose a wrong action under the strict policy, so zero-error behavior is not general.

**Potential decipherment input:** selective detection and correction of inconsistent grammatical anchor assignments in a partially solved cipher, helping maintain a usable constraint set for another search method.

Evidence: [Jerome replication, iteration 112](../grammar_jerome_context_iteration112.md), [subsequent context-loss challenge, iteration 113](../grammar_context_loss_iteration113.md).

## Current boundary

No four-text demonstration of five confidently selected Latin words per text with at least three shared correct word identities has been achieved. No trustworthy Voynich plaintext nucleus has been established. These three findings are potential grammatical inputs, with the conditions above, rather than validated components of a successful Voynich decipherment.
