# Progress: 2026-10-24

Three conditional grammatical findings may supply useful information to a future Voynich decipherment algorithm, even though they have not produced reliable lexical nuclei. Preserve them independently of whether the current decoder can exploit them. The overarching objective and working assumptions are in [high_level_goal.md](../high_level_goal.md).

## 1. Opaque function-word classification

A frozen model distinguishes prepositions (ADP) from coordinating conjunctions (CCONJ) for repeated opaque word types. On the cached source-test text, it correctly classifies **12/16 types (75%)**, versus **8/16** for raw-model and prior baselines, with **6/8 correct in each class**. Without refitting, expanded cached contexts yield **11/16 correct for Jerome (68.75%)** and **13/15 for Caesar (86.67%)**, versus eight correct for the respective baselines.

All sixteen target types are excluded from model training labels, and their identities and classes are masked during inference. However, the function-word candidate catalogue is supplied, as are surrounding anchor mappings. This is conditional binary type classification, not general token POS tagging or blind discovery of function words. Short-context transfer is uneven: Jerome's original 100 sentences yield 7/13 correct, below the prior's 8/13. References are supplied modal type classes, not token-level POS annotations.

**Potential decipherment input:** probabilistic grammatical classes for repeated cipher words, usable as constraints or features in another decoder if the required surrounding information can be obtained.

Evidence: [iteration 118](../grammar_class_validation_iteration118.md), [author transfer, iteration 119](../grammar_class_transfer_iteration119.md).

## 2. Structural PP membership from inferred opaque classes

With all target lexical identities hidden, passing inferred grammatical classes to the frozen PP-membership model increases correct selections from **23 to 235**, with false selections rising from **0 to 34**. The inferred-class result has **87.36% precision**. On the fixed originally unmapped-word cohort, correct selections rise from **11 to 174**, with **33 false selections (84.06% precision)**. Thus the class signal has measurable downstream structural value beyond class-label accuracy.

Surrounding anchor mappings remain supplied, and the test data have been reused in development. This does not recover full grammatical structure or establish blind Voynich applicability. The primary 90% precision requirement is unmet. A subsequent exhaustive diagnostic shows that a global ADP-confidence cutoff cannot satisfy both 90% precision and retention of 80% of correct selections with these fixed scores and models; this failure does not erase the underlying structural gain.

**Potential decipherment input:** soft evidence that an opaque token belongs to a prepositional phrase, which another decoder could combine with lexical, morphological, or positional constraints.

Evidence: [iteration 120](../grammar_class_pp_utility_iteration120.md), [scalar-gate limitation, iteration 123](../grammar_adp_gate_ceiling_iteration123.md).

## 3. Selective repair of corrupted supplied anchor classes

A frozen omission-aware repair policy transfers to cached Jerome context and makes **25 correct repairs, zero wrong repairs, and 39 abstentions** across **64 synthetic cross-class swaps**. Every successful repair restores the complete clean PP probability vector on the scored sample. The experiment uses 829 sentences of context while scoring the original 100 sentences; the policy also abstains on all three clean/same-class controls.

These are correlated synthetic corruption cases, not independent texts or recovered plaintext word identities. The procedure starts with largely correct supplied anchor mappings and a candidate catalogue. Restoring clean PP predictions does not make those predictions perfect: the clean scored reference has 40 correct and one wrong selected PP node. Subsequent literal-absence/context-loss tests expose a wrong action under the strict policy, so zero-error behavior is not general.

**Potential decipherment input:** selective detection and correction of inconsistent grammatical anchor assignments in a partially solved cipher, helping maintain a usable constraint set for another search method.

Evidence: [Jerome replication, iteration 112](../grammar_jerome_context_iteration112.md), [subsequent context-loss challenge, iteration 113](../grammar_context_loss_iteration113.md).

## Current boundary

No four-text demonstration of five confidently selected Latin words per text with at least three shared correct word identities has been achieved. No trustworthy Voynich plaintext nucleus has been established. These three findings are potential grammatical inputs, with the conditions above, rather than validated components of a successful Voynich decipherment.

## Follow-up: improved class model, integration tradeoff

[Iteration 124](../grammar_balanced_training_iteration124.md) improves source-test binary type accuracy from **12/16 to 14/16 (87.5%)**, AUC from **0.8125 to 0.921875**, and mean negative log likelihood from **0.61083 to 0.57430**, using class-balanced first-stage training with the same strict target-type exclusion. Frequency-weighted accuracy falls to **88.80%** because a new error affects common `que`; hard-tag downstream PP precision also falls to **80.53% (244 correct / 59 wrong)**. Preserve the class information gain as a potential decipherment input independently of this integration failure; cross-author transfer of the new model remains to be checked.

## Follow-up: frozen cross-author ranking transfer

[Iteration 125](../grammar_balanced_transfer_iteration125.md) confirms stronger expanded-context binary-class ranking on both cached authors: Jerome AUC **0.7344→0.8906**, Caesar **0.8393→0.9821**. Hard classification improves Jerome **11/16→13/16**, but regresses Caesar **13/15→11/15**, with frequency-weighted accuracy falling to **60.64%** on Caesar because common prepositions cross the fixed cutoff. Preserve the transferable ranking as conditional grammatical information, while retaining the calibration/decision failures and supplied-anchor limitations; no nucleus or general POS recovery is established.

## Follow-up: source-only context sensitivity

[Iteration 126](../grammar_class_volume_iteration126.md) finds mean absolute class-probability shifts of **18.4–19.8 percentage points at 100 sentences**, shrinking to **3.7–5.2 points at 1,600**, across three fixed source samples relative to full context. Decisions and rankings remain composition-sensitive; the thirteen-type calibration pool contains only three conjunction types. No new calibration or author tuning is adopted; uncertainty-aware structural integration is the next proposed test of whether this imperfect grammatical signal can still help decipherment.

## Follow-up: uncertainty integration has a coverage cost

[Iteration 127](../grammar_uncertain_pp_iteration127.md) averages PP probabilities over 32 type-consistent class draws. At the fixed 0.80 cutoff it yields **26 correct / zero wrong**, only three additional correct selections over unknown classes, versus hard classes **244/59**; every source-calibrated mixture fails the unchanged coverage guard. This rejects the tested integration at that cutoff, while preserving the earlier conditional class-ranking gains as potential inputs to another decipherment method.

## Follow-up: source threshold calibration recovers structural coverage

[Iteration 128](../grammar_posterior_threshold_iteration128.md) selects a posterior-mean PP threshold using source calibration only, where it gets **319 correct / 35 wrong (90.11%)**. Frozen test results improve to **271 correct / 52 wrong (83.90% precision, F1 0.4927)**, versus hard-class **244/59 (F1 0.4519)**; originally unmapped positions give **197/49 (80.08%)**. Preserve this conditional structural gain as a potential input to another decoder while recording the failed 90% precision transfer and the supplied-anchor dependency; no plaintext nuclei or default adoption.

## Milestone: useful PP structure with five supplied mappings

**Preserve [iteration 129](../grammar_anchor_dependence_iteration129.md):** with only five surrounding word mappings supplied, inferred opaque function-word classes yield **60 correct PP selections / two errors (96.77% precision)** at the pre-existing 0.80 cutoff; the matching unknown-class baseline selects nothing. The fixed originally unmapped cohort gives **30/2 (93.75%)**. The five mappings cover 5.73% of tokens and are ranked by source-fit frequency, not discovered; the result has only 7.72% PP recall, uses a supplied target catalogue, and comes from a six-budget development challenge rather than fresh validation. Zero supplied mappings reduce binary class accuracy to chance; the next proposed check freezes the five-anchor branch for cached-author replication without changing models or thresholds.

## Boundary: five-anchor author replication fails

[Iteration 130](../grammar_five_anchor_transfer_iteration130.md) freezes the source five-anchor branch on cached authors: Jerome original context gives **5 correct / zero wrong PP selections (4/0 originally unmapped)**, but expanded Jerome and both Caesar contexts give **no selections**. Class accuracy is below the majority-class prior in all four views; expanded Jerome loses its two correctly identified prepositions and Caesar predicts all target types as conjunctions. The source milestone remains recorded with this failed transfer attached; it does not establish a generally reliable sparse-anchor decipherment component.

## Diagnostic: sparse class inference is the main author bottleneck

[Iteration 131](../grammar_sparse_factorial_iteration131.md) separates class-context and PP-context budgets. With correct target classes supplied as an explicit oracle, five surrounding mappings support **Jerome 25 correct / zero wrong PP selections** and **Caesar 32/3**, including originally unmapped **22/0** and **30/0**; restoring full PP context while retaining sparse class predictions yields little. These are privileged ceilings, not recovered classes, but support testing matched sparse-context classifier training as a concrete path toward usable low-anchor grammatical information.

## Milestone: matched sparse training restores a larger Jerome subset

**Preserve [iteration 132](../grammar_sparse_training_iteration132.md):** with the same five supplied mappings, matched sparse-context classifier training raises PP selections to **source 95 correct / nine wrong (91.35%)**, **Jerome original 19/1 (95%)**, and **Jerome expanded 17/1 (94.44%)**. Originally unmapped Jerome positions give **17/1** and **15/1**, while source gives **60/8 (88.24%)**; the two Jerome views overlap and must not be pooled as independent successes. Caesar remains effectively unrecovered (**1/0**, **0/0**), so this is a useful partial grammatical finding rather than a generally transferable low-anchor solution or lexical nucleus.

## Qualification: sparse structural success is proximity filtering

[Iteration 133](../grammar_sparse_controls_iteration133.md) finds that actual classes beat all sixteen tested shuffled-class assignments on source all-token PP F1, but not all shuffles on Jerome; Caesar remains weak. Every learned PP selection is contained within the simple immediate-left-ADP rule: the model raises precision by rejecting candidates, adding no structural coverage beyond that rule and lowering F1. Preserve the 132 source/Jerome subsets as selective conditional grammar while avoiding claims of richer phrase reconstruction or reliable transfer.

## Negative addition: opaque-neighbor feature bundle fails transfer

[Iteration 134](../grammar_opaque_neighbors_iteration134.md) tests frequency-only and richer recurrence features computed solely from opaque ID equality. Recurrence raises source class accuracy **11/16→12/16**, but source PP precision falls to **86.90%**, expanded Jerome degrades **17/1→15/29**, and other author results fail; the frequency control does not rescue transfer. Reject these additions while retaining the earlier conditional sparse grammatical subsets and their proximity limitation.

## Milestone: anchor-free grammatical class signal on long Caesar context

**Preserve [iteration 135](../grammar_context_overlap_iteration135.md):** a single count-corrected directional context-overlap statistic, learned from thirteen noncatalogue types, gets **13/15 expanded Caesar types correct (86.67%)**, **AUC 1.0**, and **NLL 0.416** without supplied word mappings in its inference features. Raw-overlap and frequency controls give 7/15 and 9/15; target function-word membership and segmentation remain supplied, so this is not blind POS discovery or a plaintext nucleus. Transfer is uneven—source9/16,Jerome3/13and10/16,Caesar1006/13—and the cached Caesar result is development evidence requiring frozen volume/mixture checks.

## Milestone: long-context Caesar signal survives mixture changes

**Preserve [iteration 136](../grammar_overlap_volume_iteration136.md):** the frozen anchor-free corrected-overlap predictor gets **13/15, 15/15, and 13/15** on three 1,600-sentence Caesar mixtures, with **AUC 0.964–1.0**; on the same original thirteen types, accuracy improves from **6/13 at 100 sentences** to **11/13, 13/13, and 11/13**. The mixtures overlap and are development checks, not independent confirmations. Jerome remains unreliable at 800 sentences (**7/14, 9/16, 9/16**), so the result supports a long-Caesar grammatical signal while retaining the cross-author limitation and supplied-catalogue/segmentation privileges.

## Milestone: Caesar signal survives without sentence boundaries

**Preserve [iteration 137](../grammar_overlap_boundaries_iteration137.md):** expanded Caesar retains **13/15 binary grammatical classes correct, AUC 1.0**, after removing supplied sentence boundaries and under three fixed sentence-order variants. All sixteen global word-order shuffles preserve word counts but fall to **7/15**, with maximum AUC **0.679**. This strengthens the anchor-free word-order finding for a future decipherment component; other authors/short contexts remain uneven, candidate function-word membership is still supplied, and no exact plaintext nuclei are recovered.
